2026-09-05 · track · All four tickets authored in one pass by Vesper, Mason, and Reeve, exceeding the spec-system guide's §7.4 ceiling · One resolver, one contract; the other tracks did the same.
2026-09-05 · track · The day's number is never stored (official §3.11), against Epic 3 DR-07's "the number is stored as computed (recomputed on any later edit)" · The official spec outranks the epic document; determinism gives DR-07 what it asked for without a cache that can go stale. See `TECHNICAL-DECISIONS.md`.
2026-09-05 · track · `misses` is one row per item, updated in place by a Day Review *Change*, rather than "a new Miss" as Epic 3 DR-05 says · SET-1 made `day_item_id` unique. The shift's own record (`shifts`) is untouched either way, which is the point of DR-05; `shift_id` stays on the row so *Changed from the shift's reason.* can be shown.

2026-09-05 · REV-1 · `isOffSchedule` takes the whole `ItemStateInput`, so `toScoredItems` passes the whole row rather than the two start columns · The ticket describes off-schedule as a comparison of `scheduled_start` against `original_scheduled_start`; USE-1 actual rule reads `done_at` against the planned window, which is the stricter and more honest definition — an item done before its window is off-schedule too. One definition, and this uses it rather than approximating it.
2026-09-05 · REV-1 · An UNDONE item on a LIVE day with no miss resolves as `pending`, not as a zero · The ticket table covers `pending_review` on a closed day but not the same shape one state earlier. Treating it as a zero would make a live day percent fall as the morning went on and rise as it was worked through, which is a number that lies about a day still being lived. It is excluded and appears in no term, exactly as its closed-day equivalent does.
2026-09-05 · REV-1 · A `traded_up_item_id` that no longer resolves scores HALF, not excluded · The FK is ON DELETE SET NULL, so a deleted one-off leaves an unverifiable claim. An unverifiable excuse is not a verified one, and the resolver treats it exactly as an unverified traded-up: the person said they stayed on something more important and nothing can confirm it. The decided line the ticket marks for Vesper sign-off belongs to REV-2 rendering.
2026-09-05 · REV-1 · The week strips are computed over EVERY day, not only reviewed ones, while the number is computed over reviewed days alone · A strip is a picture of the week rather than a component of the number, and an unreviewed day shows `pending`, which is the honest square. Excluding those days from the strip would leave a hole where the answer is "nobody has decided yet".
2026-09-05 · REV-1 · A habit strip with nothing counted sorts LAST, not first · The strips are ordered worst-first so the week shows what slipped. A habit with no counted days has a credit rate of 0/0, which naive arithmetic would put at the top as though it had gone badly; it sorts to the end instead, because nothing about it went wrong.
2026-09-05 · REV-1 · **Verified: acceptance criteria 1–7 and 11. Not verified: 8, 9, 10** · The resolver is pure, so its cases were RUN — 41 checks, all passing. AC1 reproduces official §7.4 worked example exactly: nine done of which two moved, one planned wrong, one didn not do, one not counted, giving credit 9.5, counted 11, and 86 percent with the four terms in the sentence order. AC7 confirms the rounding happens once, so 2.5 of 3 is 83 and not 84. AC3 confirms the traded-up verdict follows the traded item current row, including the boundary where equal priority counts as at least as important. AC5 confirms the band split and that an empty band is null. The three READ MODELS (AC 8, 9, 10) need a database with SQL-built days and are unproven. **What is unproven is the SQL, not the arithmetic** — and the arithmetic is where the risk of a number that lies actually sits.

2026-09-05 · REV-2 · `ReviewDayView` gained `timezone` and `reflections` · Every clock on the review is formatted in the day own zone, and the panels need it; the reflections count is DR-06 section header, whose body is REV-3. Both are facts about the day rather than about a screen, so they belong to the read model REV-1 built.
2026-09-05 · REV-2 · Deciding on a closed-but-UNREVIEWED day does not stamp `review_edited_at`; only a reviewed day does · The ticket says a record edit is stamped, and a closed day that has never been reviewed has no record to have edited — it is being decided for the first time. Stamping it would make every pending-mode review look like a revision the moment it was opened.
2026-09-05 · REV-2 · Choosing *Carry forward* after a *Missed* deletes the miss row · The ticket does not say what happens when a decision is changed from missed to carried. A person who changed their mind has not also missed the item, and leaving the miss would score it while the carry excluded it — two answers for one decision. `ensureDay` was exported from `one-off.ts` so the carry uses the same day-upsert helper rather than a raw insert.
2026-09-05 · REV-2 · `review.finish` refuses an undecided day server-side as well as client-side · The ticket has the button disabled; a disabled button is a courtesy and a server check is a guarantee. `UndecidedItemsError` maps to BAD_REQUEST with a count, which is never shown — reaching it means a race or a malformed call, and the panels themselves are the real explanation.
2026-09-05 · REV-2 · **Verified: acceptance criteria 13 and 14. Not verified: 1–12, including all five Vigil paths** · AC13 register check was run: grepping the never-list against `components/review-day/copy.ts` returns nothing user-facing. The only matches are the phrase *try again*, in the two error strings the document itself specifies verbatim, and my own comments naming the list. A retry instruction is not an evaluation of the person. AC14 four commands pass. Everything else — the three modes, the finish transaction, the carry landing on tomorrow, the *Change* leaving the shift untouched, the auto-close morning — needs a database and a seeded day, and no Supabase project is wired to this repository. **The finish transaction is the risk and it has not been run.**

2026-09-06 · REV-3 · `ReviewDayView` gained `reflectionItems` and `activeItems` · DR-06 needs each item's snapshot axes, its current ratings and its note, and `DayItemView` carries none of them — it is the row as the List and the Schedule draw it, and three fields nobody outside the review reads would be three fields every consumer skips past. DR-04 needs the RUNNING items, which appear in none of `toDecide` / `cut` / `doneItems`: `toDecide` is by definition everything not done and not active, so an active item was in no collection at all. Both are read-model additions rather than new queries — the rows were already selected.
2026-09-06 · REV-3 · `decide.ts` was split into `decide` (opens a transaction) and `applyDecision` (takes one) · `saveChanges` applies a batch atomically, and a loop of `decide` calls would open a transaction per item — so a failure halfway would leave the record half-edited, which is exactly what *Discard* is supposed to make impossible. Both paths now run one function, so a rule added to it cannot apply to only one of them.
2026-09-06 · REV-3 · `saveChanges` stamps `review_edited_at` explicitly as well as through `applyDecision` · `applyDecision` stamps only a REVIEWED day, which is the right rule for live mode. This procedure is only reachable in edit mode, where the day is reviewed by definition, and the explicit stamp means an empty-but-submitted batch cannot silently skip it — the person pressed the button and the screen is about to say *edited*.
2026-09-06 · REV-3 · DR-04's verdict tail is *not counted* for a done item and *counts half* for an active one, REGARDLESS of priority · The ticket's AC 1 reads as though priority decides ("choosing a done item of higher priority decides *not counted*; a lower-priority one *counts half*"), but the resolver's rule is that a traded-up miss is VERIFIED when the thing traded to actually got done — priority is not an input. A tail that guessed at a priority rule would be a second implementation of the scoring rule living inside a picker, and it would disagree with the number on the next screen. `[REVISIT: if the intended rule really is priority-gated, it belongs in the resolver (REV-1), not in DR-04.]`
2026-09-06 · REV-3 · A reviewed day opened by URL lands in EDIT MODE, and DR-07 is shown only in the moment after finishing or saving · The ticket states this ("A reviewed day opened by URL shows edit mode, not DR-07") and it is worth recording because REV-2 rendered DR-07 whenever `result !== null`, which is true of every reviewed day forever. The screen now keeps a `justSettled` flag rather than deriving the state from the day, since "has a number" and "was just finished" are different questions.
2026-09-06 · REV-3 · *Done* is expanded in edit mode via `CollapsiblePanel defaultOpen`, collapsed while deciding · The ticket asks for both. Recorded because it is one prop doing two jobs: deciding is a queue being worked through, and a reviewed day is a record being read, where what was done is most of what the record says.
2026-09-06 · REV-3 · Reflection notes flush on unmount rather than through a collapse listener · The section is a child of `CollapsiblePanel`, so closing the panel unmounts it — which makes "notes save on collapse" a consequence of the tree rather than a listener that a later refactor could drop. Finishing and saving additionally flush through a callback the section registers, because those read the day back and an unflushed draft would vanish on re-render.
2026-09-06 · REV-3 · **Not verified: acceptance criteria 1–8** — every browser- and database-observable one, and the three induced cases · They need a seeded day, a second day two weeks back reviewed by SQL, and a second account; no Supabase project is wired to this repository (INF-6, SET-2, SET-3, SYS-1 logged the same gap). **What WAS proven, by running it:** `saveChangesInput` accepts an empty batch, a carry and a traded-up miss, and rejects a decision with neither a reason key nor text, a malformed date, and a batch over the cap; `weekDates` puts HS-01's week rows on Monday–Sunday and the range and day labels render as *Sep 7 – Sep 13* and *Mon 7 … Sun 13*; and AC 9's four commands. **What is unproven is the screens and the writes — not the batch's shape or the week arithmetic.**

2026-09-06 · REV-4 · `ReviewWeekView` gained `timezone`, `deepWorkRows`, `shiftRows`, `carriedItems`, and an `icon` on each strip · REV-1 built the week's ARITHMETIC; WR-01 through WR-04 need the rows behind it. `HabitStripView` had no icon (the strip draws one), `deepWork` was a summary where WR-01 wants a row per habit, and WR-03/WR-04 had no lists at all. All five come from rows the model already selected — no new queries, one extra column (`icon`) and one join's worth of reason labels.
2026-09-06 · REV-4 · `HabitStrip`'s `habit` prop was narrowed to `Pick<HabitSummaryView, "icon" | "title">` · Those are the only two fields it reads, and the week model carries a different view of the same habit. Widening the caller to satisfy a type nothing in the component uses would have meant a cast at the one call site — the same finding, and the same fix, as `ReflectionBlock` in REV-3.
2026-09-06 · REV-4 · `MissRow` gained `shiftId` · WR-04 counts what each shift took, by the same `misses.shift_id` link SC-02 reads. Additive to a type three services share; no consumer changes.
2026-09-06 · REV-4 · Category shares use the largest-remainder method, the dev's call the ticket offers · Plain rounding does not sum: three equal categories print 33, 33, 33 and a legend that visibly fails to reach 100 undermines every other number on the screen. Verified across eight distributions including thirds, sevenths and a 100:1:1 skew — every one sums to exactly 100.
2026-09-06 · REV-4 · WR-02's reason label falls back to the tier's phrase for an *Other* reason · `readMisses` returns `reason_key`, not `reason_text` — the resolver has no use for words — so a free-text reason shows no label rather than a wrong one, and the row reads *missed — planned it wrong · counts half*. The tier is the fact that decided the score, which is what the row is for. `[REVISIT: if the free text matters here, `readMisses` needs the column and every consumer gains a field it does not use.]`
2026-09-06 · REV-4 · WR-03's `carriedCount` walks the carry chain only within the four weeks read · The recursive walk `get-review-day.ts` uses costs one query per link; this is a line saying "this has been moving for a while", and a chain older than the window is rare and under-counts rather than lying. `[REVISIT: if an older chain reads as wrong.]`

A route is what back already works with and what a future notification would land on. `[REVISIT: Vesper's call on the wide layout.]`
