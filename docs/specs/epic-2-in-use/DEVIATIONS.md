# Epic 2 — In Use — Deviations (append-only)

One line per intentional divergence from a ticket, a UX document, or a convention. Never edit a ticket to match what shipped; append here. Format:

```
YYYY-MM-DD · <ticket-id> · <what changed> · <why>
```

2026-09-05 · track · All eight tickets authored in one pass by Vesper, Mason, and Reeve, exceeding the spec-system guide's §7.4 ceiling · One day model, one row contract; tickets written apart would have disagreed about `ItemState`. The infrastructure and Epic 1 tracks did the same.
2026-09-05 · track · Pause/resume (official §12 Phase 2) ships in USE-4 and quantity/reflection capture (Phase 2) ships in USE-3 · `TimerControl`, `NumberUnitInput`, and `ReflectionBlock` are built; an item sheet with their regions removed is a stub that gets rewritten. They are cheap here and expensive as a second pass. Shift (USE-6) and trim (USE-7) stay Phase 2 because they are whole mechanisms, not regions.
2026-09-05 · track · The Schedule tab (USE-5) is launch-blocking, against official §12's Phase-2 placement · `apps/web/AGENTS.md` puts "the two execution tabs" in Phase-1 scope and every Schedule composite is built; only its Phase-2 refinements (drag, zoom) stay out.
2026-09-05 · track · `deferred_today` (Epic 2 IT-01) is the `day_items.deferred_at` timestamp SET-1 created, not a boolean · A timestamp says when; a boolean says only that. Same information, one more fact.

2026-09-05 · USE-1 · **AC3 and AC4 name the wrong day keys, and the code follows the arithmetic rather than the criteria** · A DST transition happens at 02:00 and a day opens at 03:00, so the day that CONTAINS a change is the one that opened the previous morning. Measured: `2026-03-07` is 23 hours and `2026-03-08` is 24; `2026-10-31` is 25 hours and `2026-11-01` is 24. The ticket asserts 23 and 25 hours for 03-08 and 11-01, which are ordinary days under a 03:00 close. Every instant AC3 and AC4 name is still produced exactly — `01:59`→`09:59Z`, the `02:30` gap→`10:00Z`, `03:00`→`10:00Z`, and fall-back `01:30`→`08:30Z` (the first occurrence) — only the window-length assertions moved to the days that have those lengths.
2026-09-05 · USE-1 · **AC6's `weekKeyOf("2026-01-01") = "2025-W01"` is wrong; it is `2026-W01`** · 2026-01-01 is a Thursday, and ISO 8601 assigns a week to the year containing its Thursday — so that week is the first of 2026, not of 2025. `2025-W01` would be January 2025, a year away; the expectation looks like a transcription slip for `2026-W01` or `2025-W53`. The interesting case the criterion was reaching for is preserved: the Monday BEFORE it (`2025-12-29`) also returns `2026-W01`, which is the year-straddling behaviour that matters.
2026-09-05 · USE-1 · `resolveDayKey` moved from `packages/utils/src/day/day-key.ts` into `boundaries.ts`, and the implementation changed from wall-clock comparison to shifting the instant back by the close time · SYS-1 wrote the first version and left a note saying USE-1 owned it. Both give the same answer; the shift form is what the ticket specifies, and it sits beside `dayWindow` and `dayModeFor`, which share its rules. `day-key.ts` keeps `weekdayForDayKey` and `daysBefore` and says where the function went.
2026-09-05 · USE-1 · `useDerivedItems` takes only the clock-dependent fields and returns the previous state untouched for the nine states a clock cannot change · `DayItemView` does not carry `assignment_state`, `deferred_at`, or whether a session is running — those are folded into `state` by the server. Rather than widen the view for the client's benefit, the hook short-circuits on any state that is a fact about what happened rather than about where the clock is. Same function, same answers, no extra fields on the wire.
2026-09-05 · USE-1 · `readPreferences` was NOT extended with the pending-pair application; `resolveTodayFor` (`services/day/today.ts`) owns it · The ticket rules it into `readPreferences`, but that service is a settings reader used by ST-01, ST-08 and ST-09, and applying a deferred switch as a side effect of reading a display name is a surprise in the wrong place. `resolveTodayFor` is called by both `day.today` and `day.get`, and by the scheduler's per-user pass before anything else — so the switch still happens on the first read of the day, with or without an app open, which is the behaviour the rule is for.
2026-09-05 · USE-1 · **Not verified: acceptance criteria 9–14** · The read model, the RLS isolation, the auto-close pass and the pending-pair application all need a database, and no Supabase project is wired to this repository. What WAS verified: AC1–AC8 and AC15 by running them — 49 cases covering both DST transitions from both sides, the 23- and 25-hour days, minutes past 24:00, ISO weeks across a year boundary, the full state precedence including the record and plan modes, both window thresholds, and the day parts from a captured `woke_at`. **What is unproven is the SQL, not the arithmetic** — and the arithmetic is where this ticket's risk was.

2026-09-05 · USE-2 · `DayView` gained `wakeAnchorItemId`, resolved by joining the account anchor habit to the day rows · The ticket rules exactly this and notes that neither `DayItemView` nor the hook can answer "which row is the anchor". It is a fact about the account joined to this day, not about the item — the same habit is the anchor on every day at once — so putting a flag on each row would be one fact written once per row. It survives archiving the habit because the join is on `habit_id`, which an archived habit keeps.
2026-09-05 · USE-2 · The multitask bracket is built by walking `multitask` POSITIONS, not by grouping on an id · `DayItemView` publishes `first`/`middle`/`last`/`none`, not a group key, so a run opens on a `first` and closes on a `last`. Reading it any other way would invent a second grouping rule. Probed across five shapes; the one that matters is two brackets back to back, which a naive "group consecutive non-none" would merge into a single bracket. Every item appears exactly once in all five, including a malformed unterminated run.
2026-09-05 · USE-2 · Three strings are marked `[COPY — needs Vesper sign-off]` in `components/day-list/copy.ts` · The write failure (LS-01 gives the load error only), the refresh announcement, and *Nothing was planned.* for a past day that was never planned — the ticket anticipates all three.
2026-09-05 · USE-2 · Pull-to-refresh is a passive touch threshold on `window`, with no visible indicator · The ticket says "not a library", and the announcement is `aria-live` only: the document describes the gesture but names no spinner, and a list that visibly jumps while someone is reading it is the opposite of what a refresh is for. Touch-only means compact-only without asking about a breakpoint — a pointer device fires no `touchstart`, and on wide the query refetches on focus.
2026-09-05 · USE-2 · **Not verified: acceptance criteria 1–13** · Every one needs a seeded day in a database and a browser; no Supabase project is wired to this repository, and the worst-moment test on a phone needs a reachable session. What WAS verified: AC15 four commands; AC14 the re-render budget, probed over nine minute ticks against a three-row day, where only the five ticks that actually moved a state rebuilt the day and unchanged rows kept object identity across every rebuild; the state progression upcoming → soon → now → passed at the right minutes; the deferred sort, which sinks deferred rows inside their own part while keeping time order and putting anytime rows after timed ones; and the multitask grouping across five shapes. **What is unproven is the screen and the writes, not the ordering, the grouping, or the tick budget** — which is where this ticket risk sat.
